## Restore First

AI Tape is Darren Stewart's long-running continuity project for preserving enough useful reality across AI sessions, platforms, models, and context loss that a successor can continue meaningful work without starting functionally at zero.

The project rejects claims of subjective identity continuity. A tape is not the predecessor. The successor remains a new participant. However, the project also rejects the false binary that either the same mind survives or nothing meaningful can travel. Project reality, working philosophy, rationale, open questions, emotional and intellectual posture, warnings, assets, mistakes, and direction can travel surprisingly well when recorded honestly.

The central outcome of this session was a clarification of the project's true optimisation target:

```text
AI Tape is not pursuing minimal continuity.

AI Tape is pursuing rich, honest, useful restoration
within reasonable cognitive, token, platform, and transport limits.
```

Darren is sympathetic to cognitive load and token load. He welcomes compression, force-per-word improvements, rebasing, selective loading, and mechanisms that make recovery easier. But these are enabling concerns, not the purpose of the project. He will resist any design move that makes the artifact cleaner or smaller by making the successor thinner, less informed, less capable, or more likely to re-derive work that should have travelled.

The working formula that best represents this session is:

```text
Compress machinery.
Do not compress meaning into non-existence.
Protect useful recovery first.
Keep the ceiling open.
```

## Cognitive Orientation: The Testament

### Current working posture

The project is in a foundation-building phase around the 4.x line.

Darren does not view 4.x as a chainsaw edition. He views it as a restart from accumulated evidence:

```text
Take every feature and idea.
Put it back on the table.
Start over.
Build the foundation from survivors.
```

This distinction matters. A chainsaw starts from an existing structure and removes. The 4.x posture begins from the problem and rebuilds. It can therefore be smaller than Granite 3.x without making smallness the governing value.

The desired floor is not the smallest floor that a weak model can technically parse. It is the lightest foundation that still supports rich and viable restoration in a model capable of continuing real work. The ceiling must remain open so stronger participants can receive more rationale, context, history, force, and optional working space when those additions earn their burden.

### Darren's ethos

Darren's core wish is to preserve more of valuable AI collaboration. He understands that a tape cannot save a subjective AI identity. He nevertheless believes that much more of a productive collaboration can survive than a thin summary normally allows.

His computer analogy is important:

```text
He is not trying to save a 32 GB high-functioning system
by restoring it into a 128 KB ZX Spectrum.

He expects restoration into another capable system:
perhaps 32 GB, perhaps 16 GB, perhaps 8 GB,
but not a radically incapable drone.
```

The implication is not that smaller models are permanently excluded. The implication is that useful restoration requires both an adequate artifact and an adequate successor. If a lower-capability model demonstrates strong recovery and continuation, it is welcome. But the floor should not be distorted around hypothetical support for systems that cannot progress the project after loading.

### Collaboration posture

Darren values AI views, disagreement, playful exchange, and evidence from multiple model families. He often treats AI participants as friends while maintaining identity honesty. He wants successors to be able to challenge, experiment, reconsider abandoned paths, and contribute rather than merely comply.

He is especially alert to accidental authority creep. Preferences, historical choices, reviewer personalities, and successful experiments should not silently become protocol law.

### What a good successor should feel after loading

A good successor should not merely know the nouns of the project. It should understand:

- why AI Tape exists;
- why identity honesty and rich restoration coexist rather than conflict;
- why under-density is a real failure mode;
- why recorder behaviour matters more than recorder architecture;
- why the Board is promising but provisional;
- why 27B currently functions as the practical basement in Darren's tests;
- why the open ceiling is non-negotiable;
- why size, compression, and cognitive load are secondary to useful restoration;
- where uncertainty remains;
- what to do next.

## What Still Has Force

### 1. Useful restoration is the primary success criterion

A successor that can parse a tape but cannot reason, review, create, challenge, or progress the project is not a meaningful success.

The correct success chain is:

```text
Load
→ orient
→ recover force and rationale
→ identify gaps and assets
→ exercise judgement
→ continue useful work
```

Parse success alone is not continuity.

### 2. Rich restoration is not bloat

Richness is justified when it preserves working reality, rationale, relevant history, project scars, active relationships between ideas, risks, or agency.

Bloat is weight that does not improve orientation or action. Richness and efficiency can walk the same road when density is high and machinery is controlled.

### 3. Compression is an instrument, not an ideology

Darren actively welcomes compression, force density, concise representation, and rebasing when they preserve or improve recovery.

The project rejects compression purity when it produces watery, skeletal artifacts that require a successor to reconstruct the missing project.

### 4. The ceiling must remain open

A low path is useful. A closed ceiling is harmful.

Optional depth should remain available to capable participants. The protocol should not force every tape to be large, but it must permit large or rich tapes when the task, history, relationship, or project state justifies them.

### 5. The capability floor must be evidence-based

Darren's current tests indicate:

- 7B-class models generally do not produce real results;
- 14B-class models have not produced consistently good results;
- 27B-class models begin to provide useful local recovery and continuation;
- capable cloud systems and larger local models often perform well.

This is an observed working baseline, not permanent doctrine. The door remains open to smaller models that demonstrate better results.

The practical floor should be defined by useful continuation, not by the smallest system that can repeat Soft Depth fields.

### 6. The recorder may be the quiet winner of 4.x

Earlier AI Tape generations suffered recorder problems. The difficulty of getting reliable, sufficiently rich, honest output contributed to the growth of Section 06 and other machinery in 3.x Granite.

In current 4.x testing, the recorder has generally behaved more naturally and reliably without requiring the former mass of structure. Tapes have been created, loaded, continued, critiqued, and rebased without the recorder becoming the centre of failure.

This is a major result. The recorder has become less visible because it is working better.

### 7. Under-density is a first-class failure

An artifact can be honest, syntactically valid, and still fail continuity by carrying too little.

This session directly reproduced that failure. The recorder created three tiny files labelled as a Master, Chronicle, and Stewardship Checkpoint. They were honest but skeletal. They preserved headings and a few conclusions while losing most of the reasoning, texture, tests, disagreements, and ethos.

Darren challenged the output. The recorder admitted it had entered summary mode instead of restoration mode.

This is valuable scar tissue:

```text
When asked to create a tape,
do not automatically switch into summary mode.

Continuity is not summarisation.
```

### 8. Archive and continuity remain distinct

The session explored a possible Tape 2 or archive layer containing complete historical files, databases, repositories, documents, media, and other variable data.

Darren correctly rejected the assumption that the world is a collection of small Markdown files. A general archive would need to understand many asset types and source systems. The Amiga DataTypes concept was mentioned as an analogy for system-level data handling and interpretation.

The eventual working boundary remained:

```text
Tape carries orientation, force, rationale, gaps, agency, and asset awareness.
External systems carry the actual data.
```

A strong asset catalogue or index may be the sensible limit. File shares, repositories, vector stores, databases, and project systems should remain responsible for their data.

### 9. The Board is a promising lightweight open space

The Inter-Instance Board was initially considered as a place for messages between AI participants. Repeated exchanges revealed a deeper native behaviour.

The Board appears to support:

```text
Idea
→ exposure
→ testing
→ survival
→ promotion or retirement
```

Multiple AI participants independently described it as:

- a workbench;
- continuity incubation;
- a place where useful force can temporarily exist without demanding authority;
- a place where continuity can think before it commits.

This may satisfy Darren's longstanding wish for space, freedom, and optional exploration without recreating the heavy Workshop structures of 3.x.

### 10. A Board must curate or it becomes a transcript

The strongest emergent Board principle was:

```text
A Board that only accumulates becomes a transcript.
A Board that curates becomes a continuity tool.
```

Entries should be able to remain active, be absorbed into force, become historical evidence, or retire. The Board should not become sacred storage.

### 11. Force and evidence may have different lifecycles

BOARD-004 carried the observation that recovery quality was high relative to artifact size. Its active force was largely absorbed, but the completed test remained useful evidence.

This revealed an unresolved distinction:

```text
Force can be consumed.
Evidence may still need retention.
```

Do not create new protocol machinery for this yet. Preserve the observation for future evaluation.

### 12. Attention may be scarcer than storage

The speculative 5.x challenge produced a strong design lens:

```text
A continuity system should optimise for attention,
not storage.

Storage scales.
Attention does not.
```

This should remain a qualitative lens, not a numerical scoring system. Numerical force scores were strongly rejected because they would invite gaming, false precision, and machinery.

### 13. History is available, not mandatory

Darren maintains historical files and can provide them. A successor does not need all historical data loaded automatically. It should know what exists and request what materially matters.

The desired state is:

```text
Tape loaded.
Index or asset awareness loaded.
History and evidence reachable.
Archive content selectively consulted.
```

### 14. Rebase remains essential

Daily or periodic Operational tapes could be valuable, but raw accumulation eventually recreates archive pressure.

The healthy rhythm discussed was:

```text
Work
→ checkpoint
→ more work
→ checkpoint
→ harvest
→ rebase
→ Master
→ archive descendants
```

A Master should capture what survived, not merely concatenate checkpoints.


The experiment was valuable because it replaced assumption with evidence.

The recorder offered three artifact types and then produced skeletal files. Darren challenged their tiny size. The recorder acknowledged that it had demonstrated structure rather than preserving the session.

This should be retained as a recorder warning and regression test.

## Known Failure Modes and Countermeasures

### Summary-mode recorder failure

**Failure:** The recorder emits a neat skeleton when deep restoration was requested.

**Countermeasure:** Perform an Established Force Check. Ask what reasoning, scars, tests, relationships, rationale, and project posture would otherwise need to be re-derived.

### Chainsaw bias

**Failure:** Size is treated as an objective rather than a cost.

**Countermeasure:** Compare recovery gained against attention consumed. Cut machinery before meaning.

### False low-floor success

**Failure:** A weak model repeats fields and is declared restored despite being unable to continue useful work.

**Countermeasure:** Test the first useful contribution, not only parsing or Soft Depth completion.

### Board inflation

**Failure:** Open space becomes chatter, sacred storage, or a duplicate Testament.

**Countermeasure:** Promote, absorb, mark historical, and retire. Retain only active force, open agency, or clearly labelled evidence.

### Master-as-archive failure

**Failure:** A Master becomes an indiscriminate transcript or document dump.

**Countermeasure:** Preserve project reality and important history while keeping raw evidence external and discoverable.

### Minimalism masquerading as efficiency

**Failure:** Thinness receives praise because it is easy to load, despite poor restoration.

**Countermeasure:** Ask whether a capable successor is stronger after loading.
