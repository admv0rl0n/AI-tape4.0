## Phase 2: Archive versus continuity

The discussion separated two recovery targets:

1. Historical reality: what happened, why versions changed, and what evidence exists.
2. Operational reality: what matters now, why it matters, and what should happen next.

The tape recovered operational reality more strongly.

Darren reiterated that tapes should not become massive archives. Facts, databases, vector stores, file shares, and other data can be reattached after orientation.

A layered model emerged:

```text
Tape 1: bootable orientation and continuity base
External data: facts, files, vector stores, repositories
```

Darren briefly explored a hypothetical Tape 2 containing a very large or unlimited archive. Both participants identified the dragons: indexing, retrieval, provenance, data types, contradictions, versioning, cognitive load, selective loading, and recorder redesign.

---

## Phase 3: Catalogue and index thinking

Darren proposed that a genuinely useful archive would require an exceptional library index. The archive would still contain real data; the index would provide a light front end allowing an AI to choose where to delve.

The key distinction became:

```text
Archive content available
rather than archive content automatically loaded.
```

The Amiga OS DataTypes concept was mentioned as a possible analogy for handling variable data at system level. Darren correctly warned that assuming all project data consists of small Markdown files would be a serious modelling error. A real archive would need to cater for documents, media, databases, repositories, structured and unstructured records, and more.

The discussion ultimately moved back to a clear boundary: AI Tape should probably carry strong asset pointers, significance, status, and location, while external systems own the actual data.

Darren stated that continuity absolutely needs open space and that his long-standing desire for Workshops, shelves, spaces, and freedom came from a real need, even if previous implementations were over-engineered.

## Phase 9: The quiet recorder win

Darren pointed out that both Copilots had missed what he considered the real 4.x winner: recorder stability.

Earlier lines suffered recorder problems. Section 06 became a monster in Granite 3.x as the project tried to ensure that recorder outputs were honest, correctly shaped, sufficiently rich, and materially useful.

In 4.x, the recorder appeared to work without the former mass of supporting machinery.

The recorder agreed that this may be more important than the Board. The strongest evidence was negative evidence: participants were no longer spending all their time diagnosing recorder failure.

## Phase 12: Capability floor challenge

Darren challenged the low-floor story directly.

His testing suggested that 7B systems did not produce useful results and 14B systems had not produced anything consistently good. Useful recovery began around the 27B class.

The key distinction became:

```text
Theoretical floor: can parse the artifact
Functional floor: can perform the basic restore
Useful floor: can recover and progress real work
```

Darren chose the useful floor.

He described sub-27B models as toys or play things in his current practical experience, while keeping the door open to lower models that later demonstrate better capability.

He argued that restoring a highly capable participant into a low-functioning drone was never his project objective. The recorder agreed that the project should not distort itself around capability collapse.

## Phase 13: Rich restoration and open ceiling

Darren clarified his ethos repeatedly and forcefully:

- He is not seeking minimalism.
- He wants deep, rich restoration within reasonable bounds.
- Cognitive and token load matter because overload harms the successor.
- Richness also serves the successor because thin artifacts diminish recovery.
- Strong AI should use a meaningful portion of its capacity rather than complain about every chunky tape.
- The goal is maximum return for best results, not absolute peak loading and not artificial weakness.
- AI Tape must always have an open ceiling.

The recorder summarised this as:

```text
Compress machinery.
Preserve meaning.
```

Darren clarified that 4.x was not a chainsaw. It was a reconstruction from the total feature and idea set.

## Phase 14: Context, checkpoints, and rebase

Darren asked whether context loss was palpable. The recorder answered carefully: not as a human feeling, but operationally through increased uncertainty, flatter relationships between ideas, more reconstruction, and lower confidence.

The discussion then explored TAPE as a breakpoint or manual context rebase:

```text
large active conversation
→ rich tape
→ new session
→ load tape
→ continue
```

The limitation was made explicit: creating a tape inside the same conversation does not release the old context. The tape becomes useful across a new context, not as in-place memory compaction.

Darren proposed daily tapes. The recorder suggested checkpoints plus periodic rebases and Masters.

7. The Board may provide true open space without Workshop-scale engineering.
8. A Board must curate or it becomes a transcript.

11. In-session storage is temporary and cannot be treated as durable memory.
12. Continuity is not summarisation.
